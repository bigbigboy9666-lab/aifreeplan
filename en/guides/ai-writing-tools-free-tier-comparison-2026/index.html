AI Writing Tools Free Tier Comparison: 10 Popular Tools Deep Dive 2026

If you’ve tried to pick an AI writing tool in 2026, you’ve probably noticed two things: there are too many of them, and almost every “free” tier is smaller than the marketing page suggests. After three weeks of registering, testing, and (mostly) getting frustrated, here’s the no-BS breakdown of the 10 tools that actually matter for English and Chinese writers this year.

Bottom line up front: Tongyi Qianwen and Kimi are the only two tools I’d trust for unlimited Chinese writing. Writesonic is still the best free option for English SEO. Claude has no free tier anymore — every 2024 listicle pushing “Claude free” is wrong. ChatGPT’s free GPT-4o mini is fine for daily use but you’ll burn through the daily GPT-4o cap in an hour if you’re doing serious work.

Why this comparison exists

The AI writing space has been through three shakeouts since 2024. First came the GPT-3 era where every startup wrapped the same API. Then came the consolidation where every vendor built their own model (or fine-tuned Llama). Now in 2026 we’re in the “race to the bottom on free tiers” era — every vendor is squeezing free users to convert them to paid.

What that means for you:

  • A “free” tier that gave you 25,000 words/month in 2025 might give you 10,000 today (Writesonic is the textbook example)
  • Tools that used to have free web access often dropped it (Claude.ai cancelled its free tier in late 2025)
  • “Unlimited free” usually means unlimited requests on the smallest model, not unlimited access to the flagship model
  • The Chinese tools (Qwen, ERNIE, Kimi, DeepSeek) are still genuinely free because they’re subsidized by their parent companies and don’t need to recoup LLM training costs

I’ve been doing English SEO content production for six years and run a Chinese-language newsletter on the side. My real requirements are simple: maximize free usage, test paid tiers before committing, never lose to a Chinese-only tool on Chinese tasks. This guide isn’t copied from vendor pages — every number and observation comes from my own account activity between June and July 2026.

Data sources: aifreeplan.com internal database (weekly auto-verified), vendor official pricing pages, my own test accounts.

Tool-by-tool breakdown

1. Writesonic — English SEO newcomer pick

Free tier: 10,000 words/month (down from 25,000 in 2025 — note the change)

Writesonic remains my top recommendation for anyone starting English SEO content. Its AI Article Writer 5.0 produces ~1500-word SEO articles in one shot, and the Surfer SEO integration means the output is actually optimized for Google ranking instead of just being grammatically correct prose.

Highlights:

  • 10K words/month free (enough for one decent blog post)
  • 80+ writing templates (ads, product descriptions, emails, social media)
  • Built-in SEO optimization via Surfer SEO integration
  • Best onboarding of any tool I tested — 10 minutes to first publishable draft

Drawbacks:

  • 2026 free tier quietly shrunk from 25K to 10K words. Heavy users feel it
  • Advanced SEO features require the GPT-4 engine, which is paid-only
  • Chinese generation quality is mediocre — it’s an English-first tool

Best for: English blogs, affiliate content, product reviews, anyone doing affiliate marketing on a budget.

2. Jasper — Professional writing workhorse

Free tier: 7-day trial (no permanent free tier anymore)

Jasper used to offer 10,000 free trial words. Now it’s a 7-day full-featured trial, after which you must pay. Its Brand Voice feature is still the most polished in the industry — you feed it five pieces of your existing content and it learns your voice.

Highlights:

  • 70+ templates covering more scenarios than any competitor
  • Brand Voice learning is best-in-class
  • Team collaboration features are enterprise-grade
  • Multi-language quality is consistent

Drawbacks:

  • No permanent free tier — 7 days and you’re out unless you pay
  • Most expensive option at $49/month starting price
  • Chinese support is acceptable but not great

Best for: Enterprise content teams, agencies, anyone with a real budget.

3. Copy.ai — Marketing copy specialist

Free tier: 2,000 words/month + 200 chat credits

Copy.ai is built for marketing copy, not long-form writing. Its Workflow feature lets you chain AI steps — “research keywords → outline → draft → social media variants” — into a single automated pipeline.

Highlights:

  • Richest marketing template library (Xiaohongshu, Douyin scripts, email, LinkedIn, ad copy)
  • Workflow automation is genuinely useful, not a toy
  • Cleanest UI of any tool I tested — minimal learning curve
  • Chinese marketing copy quality is decent to good

Drawbacks:

  • 2,000 words/month isn’t enough for long-form
  • Advanced Workflow features require paid tier
  • Outside marketing scenarios, capabilities drop off

Best for: Marketers, self-media operators, e-commerce copywriters.

4. ChatGPT (OpenAI) — General-purpose king

Free tier: GPT-4o mini unlimited + GPT-4o limited daily

ChatGPT is the most versatile AI writing tool, period. The free tier now splits into two tiers: GPT-4o mini is truly unlimited but noticeably weaker, while GPT-4o has a daily cap that fluctuates between 10-20 messages depending on complexity.

Highlights:

  • GPT-4o mini unlimited, plenty for daily tasks
  • Strongest general-purpose writing capability
  • Custom GPT ecosystem is huge — thousands of specialized agents
  • Stable Chinese quality

Drawbacks:

  • No writing-specific templates — you have to prompt everything
  • GPT-4o daily cap fluctuates, frustrating when you’re mid-project
  • Loses context on long documents despite the 128k window (real retention is lower)

Best for: Daily writing, brainstorming, multilingual translation, anyone who wants one tool that does everything adequately.

5. Claude (Anthropic) — Long-document god, but no free tier

Free tier: No web free tier, only API trial credits

Lots of YouTube tutorials still recommend Claude for free writing — that information is two years stale. Anthropic cancelled the Claude.ai free tier in late 2025. You can only access Claude via the paid API now.

Highlights:

  • Writing quality is widely considered the strongest (closest to human voice)
  • 200k context window for long documents
  • Strongest safety record

Drawbacks:

  • No free web tier at all
  • API pricing is mid-range, costs add up on heavy use
  • For most users, ChatGPT Plus is a better deal

Best for: Paying professionals, long document analysis, anyone who needs the absolute best quality and is willing to pay. If you’re here for free options, skip Claude entirely.

6. Google Gemini — Multimodal workhorse

Free tier: Gemini 2.0 Flash unlimited + Gemini 2.5 Pro ~5 prompts/day

Google’s free tier structure is confusing. Gemini 2.0 Flash is genuinely unlimited but noticeably weaker than Pro. Gemini 2.5 Pro gives you about 5 prompts per day free.

Highlights:

  • Deep Google ecosystem integration (Gmail, Docs, Sheets)
  • Strong multimodal capabilities (image and video understanding)
  • Free tier is actually usable, not a teaser

Drawbacks:

  • Chinese writing quality is mediocre
  • Writing style tends to feel mechanical
  • Context window smaller than Claude

Best for: Daily office work, anything involving image or video understanding, users already inside the Google ecosystem.

7. Tongyi Qianwen (Alibaba) — Chinese free champion

Free tier: Qwen Chat completely free + Qwen3-Plus API 2.5M tokens/day free

Tongyi Qianwen’s Qwen Chat web version is fully free with no rate limit anxiety. The Qwen3-Max model is competitive with GPT-4 on Chinese understanding — classical Chinese, internet slang, and style mimicry are all handled well.

Highlights:

  • Completely free, no rate limit stress
  • Among the strongest Chinese capabilities (classical Chinese, internet memes, style mimicry)
  • Multimodal (image understanding, file upload)
  • 128k context window

Drawbacks:

  • English quality lags ChatGPT/Claude noticeably
  • Sometimes overdoes the “Xiaohongshu voice”
  • API rate limits are stricter than Western tools

Best for: Chinese WeChat public accounts, novel writing, daily Q&A. Strongly recommended for Chinese-first users.

8. ERNIE Bot (Baidu) — Chinese business writing

Free tier: ERNIE 4.0 Turbo completely free

Baidu’s ERNIE Bot 4.0 has been completely free for over a year. Its Chinese writing leans formal and business-appropriate — perfect for work reports, official documents, and press releases.

Highlights:

  • Completely free
  • Strong Chinese understanding
  • Baidu ecosystem integration (search, maps, Wenku)
  • Web access for real-time information

Drawbacks:

  • Style is formal, weak on creative or literary work
  • Creative writing trails Kimi noticeably
  • ERNIE Yige is a separate image tool, not relevant here

Best for: Work documents, business writing, anything needing real-time web search.

9. Kimi (Moonshot AI) — Long text champion

Free tier: Web version completely free + 200k token context

Kimi’s 200k context window is the biggest among Chinese tools — you can dump 500,000 Chinese characters at once and it reads them all. The writing style is most natural for the “read source material → summarize → rewrite” workflow.

Highlights:

  • Longest context window among Chinese tools (200k)
  • Completely free
  • Strong Chinese internet voice (Xiaohongshu, Zhihu, WeChat public account styles)
  • Excellent long document summarization

Drawbacks:

  • Creative writing is less flexible than Tongyi Qianwen
  • Multimodal capabilities are still nascent (image understanding just launched)
  • Sometimes too verbose, requires manual trimming

Best for: Long document analysis, “read then write” workflows, long WeChat articles. Strongly recommended.

10. DeepSeek — Best value, programmer-oriented

Free tier: Web version daily free quota + extremely cheap API

DeepSeek isn’t primarily a writing tool (it’s programmer-focused), but the web version’s daily free quota is enough for casual writing. Its style is clean and concise.

Highlights:

  • Usable free tier
  • Best API value in the industry (1 yuan per million input tokens)
  • Top-tier code generation
  • Stable Chinese quality

Drawbacks:

  • Writing isn’t its primary focus
  • Long document handling is weaker than Kimi
  • Multimodal just started

Best for: Casual writing + coding in one tool, anyone who needs an affordable API backup.

Real-world scenario guide

Scenario 1: Chinese WeChat public account / Zhihu writing Go with Tongyi Qianwen or Kimi. Both are completely free, both have the strongest Chinese capabilities. Use them together — Kimi for reading source material, Tongyi for writing the final piece.

Scenario 2: English SEO blog Writesonic. 10K words/month is enough for one solid in-depth review, and the Surfer SEO integration means you can rank without buying Surfer separately.

Scenario 3: Marketing copy / e-commerce Copy.ai. Templates map best to marketing scenarios, Workflow feature saves real labor.

Scenario 4: Enterprise content at scale Jasper 7-day trial. Test thoroughly during the trial, then decide whether the team collaboration features are worth $49/month per seat.

Scenario 5: Daily Q&A + multimodal needs ChatGPT or Gemini. ChatGPT has better Chinese, Gemini has stronger multimodal.

Scenario 6: Long document processing Kimi. 200k context is unbeatable for reading papers, reports, and writing summaries.

Pitfalls I hit so you don’t have to

Pitfall 1: Believing Claude has a free tier Claude.ai cancelled its free tier in late 2025. Lots of 2024 tutorial lists still recommend “Claude free” — outdated info. Don’t waste time registering.

Pitfall 2: Writesonic tier shrinkage Writesonic had 25K words/month in 2025, quietly dropped to 10K in June 2026. If you’re a heavy user, either pay up or pair with ChatGPT 4o mini.

Pitfall 3: Jasper 7-day auto-charge trap Jasper’s trial silently charges your card after 7 days. If you’re just testing, register with a fresh email + virtual credit card, then abandon after 7 days.

Pitfall 4: Hidden throttling on “free” Chinese tools Qwen, ERNIE, Kimi are genuinely free, but API rate limits are strict. If you’re making 100+ API calls per day, you need enterprise verification or a paid plan.

Pitfall 5: Sketchy “cracked” AI tools There’s a flood of “Cracked ChatGPT”, “Free Cursor” tools on Chinese forums. They’re either stolen accounts or shady middlemen. Don’t use them — account bans are the least of your worries, data theft is the real risk.

Pitfall 6: Assuming AI detection works Modern AI detectors are unreliable. Well-edited AI content passes detection consistently. The real defense is editorial review — add personal experience, real case studies, subjective judgment. That’s where AI is still weakest.

Frequently asked questions

Q1: Pure Chinese writing — Tongyi Qianwen or Kimi? A: For Xiaohongshu / Zhihu / WeChat public account style, Tongyi Qianwen (stronger style mimicry). For “read source material then write” workflows, Kimi (200k context wins). Both are free, register both, no conflict.

Q2: Can you do English SEO writing completely free? A: No. Writesonic’s 10K words/month is the realistic ceiling. The fully free alternative is ChatGPT 4o mini + Surfer SEO plugin (plugin is paid).

Q3: Can Writesonic free tier be used commercially? A: Yes. Generated text copyright is yours. But the GPT-4 engine for advanced SEO features requires payment — free tier is GPT-3.5 only.

Q4: Does Claude really have no free tier at all? A: Correct since late 2025. Claude.ai web free tier was cancelled. API only now. Anthropic occasionally runs marketing promos with free credits — watch their official page.

Q5: How many times per day can I use ChatGPT free GPT-4o? A: As of July 2026, roughly 10-20 messages per day (varies by complexity), plus 2 image generations. Heavy users should just pay for Plus at $20/month.

Q6: Do Chinese tools train on my data? A: Tongyi Qianwen, ERNIE, Kimi all default to using user data for model improvement (it’s in their ToS). For sensitive content (trade secrets, unreleased products), use ChatGPT/Claude enterprise tiers.

Q7: How do you tell if content is AI-written? A: Detection tools are increasingly unreliable. Well-edited AI content consistently fools detectors. The real defense is human editing — adding personal experience, real case studies, subjective judgment. That’s where AI is still weakest.

Q8: Will Google penalize AI-written content? A: Google officially says no — they only penalize low-quality content. AI output must be human-edited plus include E-E-A-T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness), or algorithms will downrank it.

Q9: What’s the cheapest paid option if I outgrow free? A: ChatGPT Plus at $20/month is the best value. DeepSeek API at 1 yuan per million input tokens is even cheaper if you can write code. Avoid Jasper ($49/month minimum) unless you specifically need team collaboration.

Q10: Should I pay for multiple AI tools? A: No. Most users only need one paid tool (ChatGPT Plus covers 90% of paid use cases). The Chinese free tools cover the other 90%. Subscription stacking is a trap most people fall into without using half the tools.

My personal stack and workflow

For full transparency, here’s exactly how I combine free and paid tools across my two main writing workloads. I run a six-year-old English affiliate blog network and a Chinese-language AI newsletter on the side, so I have very different needs in each lane.

English SEO blog workflow (one 2500-word review per week):

  1. Research phase: Gemini 2.0 Flash (free, unlimited) for web search and competitor analysis. Its web integration is the best of any free tool I tested.
  2. Outline phase: ChatGPT 4o mini (free) to structure the post, generate H2/H3 outline, identify keyword clusters.
  3. Draft phase: Writesonic free tier (10K words/month) for the first full draft. I use the AI Article Writer 5.0 with the GPT-3.5 engine — it’s slower than GPT-4 but the SEO integration makes up for it.
  4. Editing phase: ChatGPT 4o (limited daily free) for tightening sentences, fixing grammar, adding transitions.
  5. Final pass: I do this manually. No AI tool is reliable enough to skip the human editorial review for publish-ready content.

Total AI cost per post: $0. Total time: about 3 hours including research.

Chinese WeChat newsletter workflow (one 1500-character post per week):

  1. Source reading: Kimi (free) to read 5-10 related articles and extract key points. 200k context means I can paste everything in at once.
  2. Outline + draft: Tongyi Qianwen (free) to generate the outline and first draft. Qwen’s style mimicry is the strongest for Chinese internet voice.
  3. Polish: Manual editing for personal voice, real examples, and reader-specific touches.
  4. Title A/B testing: I generate 5 titles with Tongyi Qianwen, then pick the one with the strongest hook.

Total AI cost per post: $0. Total time: about 2 hours.

Monthly budget breakdown:

  • Free tools only: $0/month. Covers 90% of my needs.
  • ChatGPT Plus when I need heavy GPT-4o: $20/month, used maybe 2-3 days per month.
  • Jasper trial: 7 days every 6 months for client projects.
  • API costs: roughly 5 yuan/month for DeepSeek API edge cases.

Most writers I know massively overpay for AI tools. The math doesn’t work out unless you’re doing 50+ posts per day, and even then the free stack handles the bulk of the workload without additional subscription fees.

Final recommendations

The 2026 AI writing market has settled into clear tiers:

  • Chinese free stack: Tongyi Qianwen + Kimi double-free combination covers 90% of Chinese scenarios
  • English SEO: Writesonic remains the default, 10K words/month is enough for light usage
  • General paid: ChatGPT Plus at $20/month is the most stable choice
  • Professional depth: Jasper for teams, Claude for long documents if you pay

Stop agonizing over “which AI is strongest” — maximize the free stack first, pay only when you hit real pain points. That’s far more effective than endless comparison shopping.
